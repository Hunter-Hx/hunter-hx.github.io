Chapter Two

Names Wes

Fiction • 5 min read • Updated Mar 2026

Ted was in the dark as he tried to collect his thoughts. How the news of his capture was going to break his younger sister. With the small light that crept into his room, he could see a spider; a black widow. It hung with its limbs as it was trying to incapacitate its victim. He watched closely and knew what black widow spiders meant in their world, it signified suffering and later death. Perhaps that was a way his creator was inviting him to join him in the last dance.

He was drawn to watching the spider that he missed to notice that Sam had opened his door.

When he came back to his senses, he saw two figures behind Sam. He didn’t know who they were and what they wanted. They stood like statues. Like they were being controlled by some remote. They stared blankly. For two minutes, no one uttered a word.

As they say, in prison time blends so that sometimes you forget to notice what day or even time it is. Through the door and the crevices that the figures created, Ted could see it was dusk but he couldn’t figure out the exact time. He was sure the time for his meal hadn’t reached and so seeing them meant one thing; trouble.

These prisoners had paid Sam a small token so they could work on Ted. Someone had paid them to torture him and since that’s what they lived for; it was one golden opportunity. The black widow spider was a message.

Dan hit the grills of his cell with his truncheon making a short deafening sound that brought Ted back to his senses.

“Mr. Chicko, these friends want to have a little chat with you,” he said taking out his stained white handkerchief to wipe off a non-existent sweat. Ted didn’t say a word because even though he recognized that name, he assumed it wasn’t him. Only one person called him by that name, his sister who was a teacher.

“Hey convict, I’m talking to you,” Dan raised his voice. The three other men still stood silently like they weren’t bothered by anything.

“Who told you that name?”
“Listen con, I’m not here to answer your questions.”
“There it is, a con.”

Ted preferred the name Con over Chicko and when Sam addressed him with what he hated the most, he knew someone had been talking. And that could only mean one thing, his sister. But he never wanted to jump to conclusions. He assumed that it was just a random name that Sam came up with to address him. He let it flood away.

The three men behind seemed to have lost patience; they frowned their faces showing that they weren’t interested in the pampering Sam was doing.

“Badge, let’s do what brought us here,” a slightly hasty-sized man started.

“You don’t command me in my own house, you are a convict just like our little friend here,” he said pointing at Ted. “I’m still chatting with Mr. Ted.”

Dan moved a step closer to Ted and took out his handcuffs. He added them on top of the one Ted already had. He lifted him and matched him out to the waiting goons.

“My friends are going to have a little chat?”
“What little chat? I don’t want problems, boss.”
“No! No! No problem. They are taking you to the prison's general population to make a few friends.”
“I don’t want friends. I’m dying in a few days and there’s no essence for friends.”

“You are right but you must come with us or I will drag you. It’s not a request Mr. Ted,” his voice was final. Ted agreed to come with them. But there was a problem.

Among the three men, Ted seemed to recognize one. His name was Mutinda and he was Robert’s former hitman. Then and there, Ted crossed the t’s and dotted the i’s. He was being taken to an abattoir. And Mutinda was going to do the job.

Ted did what any normal human would do- prepare. And since death had become his friend on the wait, unnoticeably, he had taken the fork he used the previous day into his pocket. He was ready for anything. When death hugs you, the only thing you can do is hug back.

When they got to the general population, he pled with Sam to uncuff him and for the first time, Sam agreed to his request and left leaving him all exposed and vulnerable.

All the other convicts started yelling at him. They were chanting as everyone wanted to lay their hands on one man who had the guts to kill a deputy governor. Some supported him for his actions while others wanted to drop him dead. The three men didn’t say anything until they heard what he said. Unknowingly, Ted had shouted, “I didn’t do it.” And that landed him in more trouble.

“What did you say?” Mutinda started. The crowd was starting to build behind them.

“I haven’t said anything. Please, you don’t have to do this,” Ted tried to plead but no one was going to listen to his lies.

“I support you, Ted, that deputy governor was a pain in the neck. Thanks to you he’s no more,” one voice came from amidst the building crowd. Before Ted could raise his eyes to see who it was, a knock hit him in the head that sent him to the floor. The crowd was building fast. HPW Walls had 357 prisoners and three-quarters of that number were in the general population. News of Ted spread fast more than a woman could call over the fences.

Down where he had fallen, the three guys were working on him. They stepped on him with their metal boots. They punched him as they yelled.

Dan left for home after intentionally taking Ted to the beasts. The guards on shift therefore had no idea what was going on. When they saw the commotion and the noise, it was time to act. They threw teargas canisters as they tried to rescue Ted who was bleeding from the beatings.

When the crowd finally dispersed and order was restored, Michael took Ted back to where he belonged. On the way, he tried to figure out how Ted ended up in the general population.

“My friend, how did you even get there?” Michael asked with concern.

“It was Dan.”
“Dan?”
“Yeah, he came with these three guys that wanted a little chat and that’s when he left me there, to be killed, I guess.”
“No! No! That’s not ethical and he knows it.”
“Well, he ignored it anyway.”
“I’ll get you a doctor,” Michael locked the door behind him. He at least always switched on the room light for Ted. He didn’t have the hatred that his colleagues had.

Dan was the guard’s leader and he was rogue. Michael, therefore couldn’t ask what he did to Ted unless he wanted to be jobless. The matter therefore vanished like it never happened.
