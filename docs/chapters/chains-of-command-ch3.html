Chapter Three

Evil Siz

Fiction • 5 min read • Updated Apr 2026

My name is Ted but my sister calls me Chicko. Probably because I’m the only one light in my family. She’s dark, my mom’s dark and so is my younger sister. My dad, I never really saw him. Mom says he died when I was born. He died of shame for bearing an albino child, me. That makes me feel that he was dark too, probably very dark. His friends criticized him and told him to kill the albino child. “That’s a curse to your family,” they said. Even his closest friend had ditched him claiming that if he gets too much contact with my father, he could bear an albino too. Those people were so naïve that they thought a light-skinned kid was an albino. They called me a yellow potato.

“What has befallen you has no cure,” the village elder had said.

With shame, fear, and trauma, he committed suicide, because of me. My skin. Sometimes I wish I was dark too, so my sister could have nothing to love about me.

My younger sister has a different father but Sarah loves her because they share the same skin color. I love her too. She’s the comedian of our family. When she was born, I hoped that maybe she’ll be light-skinned too so I could feel like a member of my family. I was very disappointed when she became darker and darker as she grew up. But her skin is nothing, she’s my sister, and she’ll always be.

Sarah, my sister was fascinated by one thing about me, my skin. I presume that’s the only thing she ever loved me for. She’d walk with me just to show her friends how nice I looked. For sure, my skin was glowing. I didn’t have any acne or a blemish on my face. My mom ensured my skin stayed protected. And could bath me with warm water with much caution.

When I was 6, my mom had left for the market to pick up some grub. Sarah and I were left at home and she was to bathe me. She boiled water, added a little cold, and dipped me in. I felt my skin burning as I jumped out of the fiery basin towards the house. I didn’t like what she had done to me. When my mom returned home, Sarah had disappeared. I hated her but I couldn’t hate her forever. She was our blood or at least I was their blood.

Living as an albino was tough. Everyone thought I was some ghost. Every parent in the village didn’t want me to interact with their kids. “He’ll give you the curse,” mothers would yell. I had nowhere to go but to sit alone at home. I had no friends. I was alone and trust me, loneliness is the safest place I know. It gives you the silence your soul needs, it kept me from the unnecessary yapping of mothers. My mom always left for the market and Sarah would leave after her, leaving me alone.

I enjoyed being alone as I got to hold the two things I was obsessed with; knife and fire. I loved having a knife. Just playing with it. I had no ill intention of killing anyone. Even though I loved knives, I feared seeing blood. It made my body shiver.

As I aged, I got used to everything. I knew it was just a few more days before it passed.

As I said, Sarah was obsessed with my skin. I hated what she always did to me. She’d walk her fingers on my body. I knew she was a stupid sister but she was my elder sister. I had to do anything she wanted when mom wasn’t around. And Mum was always not around which meant I got to spend more time with her. She knew I had no friends so staying home was the best I could do. Sit. Alone.

Since she had grown, she had her room. I was very happy not to share a room with her again. I could clean my room and arrange everything and Mum loved me for that. I was clean.

One day, she intentionally took out her bed to clean it. I was 17 and so I was strong enough to hold a couple of things. She pretended to wash her bed. Let it out to dry and mopped her room. When she was done with the cleaning, she called me to help her take in her bed. I hated going into her room but since she needed my help, and I was the only help she got, I gave in.

After we returned everything, she arranged her room and asked me to get her a glass of water. When I got in, she closed the door behind me. I was terrified. Why’s she doing this? Why close the door…

I hate to talk about what happened thereafter.

“Don’t tell anyone anything,” she stated
“Why did you do this?” I asked
“I like your skin,”
“Then you can make yours look like mean. They sell bleaches, don’t they?”
“Yeah, but I couldn’t rest until my breast is pressed upon it, Chicko,”
“What will mom say?”
“She’ll only know if you tell her. And if you do tell her, I’ll kill you.”
“You’d have looked for someone else, not me.” I was getting more annoyed.
“There was no one, and you are not my brother.”
“What?”
“Mum will never tell you this.”

First, I hated what she had called me. Chicko. Which meant I was her side chick. She was 21 when I was 17. Fuuuck! I hated that name.

Second, I wasn’t her brother. Maybe she was correct. I asked myself too if she was my sister. The skin color said it, everyone must have doubted if I belonged to that family.

I was quiet for long. I felt downgraded. I couldn’t find answers to the what-ifs. It haunted me, my life and it still does. I never told anyone. I kept it all to myself, even if it meant drowning and dying for it. My little secret couldn’t be out.

And there it was, she named me Chicko. Her sex toy.

When you often do something, you get used to it, it becomes a routine. You can’t go a long time without it. I got used to what she was doing to me. Humiliation. Sex. And then she’d get me a little gift, a consolation, maybe. To fatten up for tomorrow. To energize for the future. To increase the curls from one to two to three… it was all day long. I had become what I never wanted to be. But I had no choice. I had nothing… and I wasn’t her brother.

Honestly, I hated it in the beginning but later on got used to it. It was as easy as drinking coffee or biting an ice cream. Ice cool. It relieved my stress, maybe.

It went on until the day she was recruited as a teacher. I will never forget the night before she left for her job. It’s planted in my memory with super glue. Ten curls! She left my pocket full. I didn’t know where she got all the money from. I wasn’t interested in knowing either. Why would I?

We kept Mom in the dark. I bet that’s the place she wanted to be. But Sarah was still my sister.

My actions began haunting me later. It was evil. Vice. Unfilthy. I didn’t know what her motives were until…

* * * * * * *

On the previous day before I got caught, she had called me. We had gone three months without talking. I saw her call and knew something was wrong. I stared at the phone for a while. Could she be asking me to do the act again? Was she trying to justify that what we had done was right? I held back and finally answered it. “Hey Chicko, I need you to do something for me,” she started.

“What exactly?” I asked, a little terrified.
“Just a favor, Chicko,” I growled. I hated that name.
“What favor?” asked uninterestedly.
“A job,”

She called a job a favor. That should have raised my wits high but I wasn’t that smart. I trusted her. And I needed something for an income.

Desperation made me my cross.

Following what happened years back, I need to listen to her job theory.

She told me someone was waiting for me at Ncazi, our market. It was a thirty-minute walk but I could always cover the distance in half that time. I was to pick something in a bag and take it to a warehouse that was another 15 minutes away from Ncazi and from there she was to guide me on what to do. I had set my phone’s ringtone so loud, I wanted to receive a call within minutes. I couldn’t let my job slip away.

When I got to the market, I started tracing the guy. I had been given his number. Took me 5 minutes to get him. I had never seen that guy. He was huge, probably 7’1. His muscles showed out and his Adam’s apple was so big that you’d think he was keeping a store of apples in his throat. He was sweaty and had probably been running to get on time. I was terrified. I was lost, but above all, I saw my death walking right into my arms cuddling me into its arms, trying to comfort me not to be afraid. He handed me the bag and told me not to open it. “We are done here. Your sister will tell you what’s next,” his voice roared.
“She already told me,” I picked up the bag and walked away.

My sister is a teacher. I don’t know which school exactly. How did she end up doing business with that psycho, I asked myself. Something was wrong. Something was wrong. “Don’t open the bag,” I could hear his voice echoing within my brain every minute.

I was curious to know what I was carrying but I had been warned not to open it. However, I could feel what was inside. Two guns. I walked to the warehouse and coincidentally, she called. I don’t believe in coincidences; I had been trapped. She handed the phone to someone else who gave me these instructions.

“I want you to walk toward the red sedan car on your left. Take out the guns, drop the shell casings on the floor, and hold them two in your hands.”

I did exactly that.
“Someone’s coming to pick them up?” I asked.
“Actually, no,” she replied
“Can I talk to my sister?”
“Your sister?

Oh my God, my knees felt weak as I nearly dropped down the floor. I could still make it right. I stayed up to listen to her words over the phone. She hung up.

Within seconds, police officers were all over the place and I couldn’t walk out with guns in my hands. My fingerprints are all over them. I thought I would just be thrown down and they’d take those guns and leave me. They didn’t. One officer, who seemed to be their leader walked to the sedan, and in it was a dead body with gun shorts. I was the killer. Killer of the deputy governor.

Ballistics report showed that the bullets were actually from the 9mm guns I was carrying and with no CCTV cameras to prove the findings, I had become a murderer. I was convicted of murder.

And now here I am at HWP Walls, waiting to be executed having been framed by one sister that we fucked with, who we knew everything about each other. The truth is, I don’t know her. She acted smart to make me believe that I knew her when I knew nothing about her. My mom doesn’t know anything about what happened. I have no hopes of getting out of here. I can’t plan my escape. I can’t do anything. I’m caged, and my sister has paid some people to ensure I never get out alive.
