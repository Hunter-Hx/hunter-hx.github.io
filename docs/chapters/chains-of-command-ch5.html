Chapter Five

Akinyi

Fiction • 5 min read • Updated Apr 2026

My name is Jackline. Jackline Akinyi. My brother calls me Akinyi. He loves that name and I love it too. I’m 14 years old. My only brother is scheduled to die in six days. I can do so little to delay or even cancel his execution. I must try to make sure they don’t kill my only brother. I love him and if they kill him, they kill me. He has always been my joy giver. He’s teasing but smart. He calls me the comedian of the family.

I know the government can barely listen to me, I’m sure of that, but I must try. It’s trying and failing that brings up everything we see or use. My case is different though, I fail and they kill him. I can’t afford to lose. My chances of winning are so thin but I have a little hope. I’ve always had hope. I’ve always had plans so I believe I can pull this thing off. I can save my brother.

I’m planning on using the law that they created to fight them. I want to add my brother one more day or two, it’ll all depend on how my plan goes. They aren’t going to just kill my brother as I sit and watch. No!

Being in high school, I’ve gathered some physics and chemistry that I hope may help. I’m not planning on breaking him out. No! That’s impossible at HPW Walls. The prison was designed by the best of the best, with walls full of sensors that sound an alarm when someone gets nearer. I wasn’t going to risk that. And I don’t have time for it. I want a clean and smart operation.

Yesterday when I went to visit my brother, I studied the area and I couldn’t see any mistakes in the prison. But no matter how perfect something is, it must have a flaw. So, I decided to ask about a standby generator. That’s the only flaw the prison had. No standby generator. They trusted their source of power and I loved that. Trust can fuck us up sometimes as I’m about to show them. I’m not hundred percent sure that it’ll work, but like I said, I have faith.

I have managed to use a fake insight account to enter into the net and download our constitution. It’s a crime in my state to do that, but I have no choice. Only the working class and people with ID are allowed to have the constitution. To have an ID, one must be registered with the government on the oath that you’ll never ridicule, oppose, or even say anything against the government. My mother has never vowed to follow all that and so, we aren’t entitled to catch a glimpse of the constitution. It’s not even taught in schools. We are not working class so we have no right to see the Constitution, too. That makes me wonder why it exists in the first place. It should be for the people but here, it is for the working class. I’ve committed the first crime to save my brother. I don’t care as long as they don’t notice, which they won’t, I’m safe.

My mom has lost every hope to live. I don’t know what’s going on in her mind. She’s sullen and glum. I understand and so I try not to talk of it as much.

As far as I can remember, my sister Sarah had a slippery relationship with my brother Ted. I’m not sure how weak it was but they barely talked when I was around. I don’t know what was between them. Maybe one wanted the other. I don’t know and surely, that is irrelevant at the moment. I need her help. Schools are closed so I bet she must be free to offer anything of substance. I need to call her and tell her what I bet she already knows. I need to tell her that; “Hey, we need to get our brother out.” I don’t know what her response is going to be.

I’ve put my brother’s life in front of everything. His life is hanging by a thread.

For the two days I’ve been home, I’ve gathered all the information that I need. Everything I have about conviction and execution. I’ve read the Constitution and printed it for reference. I have gathered a little about electricity. I’m hoping that a combination of electricity and law could help me. I hope it will save my brother, Ted. I hate to think that every time I think of him, he’s in the dark with some thin, almost dying guard watching over him. The thought of him keeps me awake all night. I open the internet to learn anything I have to learn about electricity. You probably wondering how electricity is going to save my brother. The thing is, I’m asking myself the same thing.

There must be two ways to approach this situation. I’m halfway through the first plan and it’s time to undertake the second one. So, I’ve decided to visit Robert. He isn’t going to give me what I ask for but this could help, at least.

At Robert’s, the guards are yawning as they see me approaching. They think I’m some lost kid. Yes, I’m a kid but I’m not lost. I’ve come for justice which I’m pretty sure I won’t get.

“Can I see Mr. Robert, please,” I enquire.
“What brings a kid here, huh?”
“I want to talk to Robert,” I insist.

“Hey kid, chill. This is not a post office where you can just walk in at your own will. We have rules here,” one short guard says as he tries to hold me down. I hate him already. He’s so much focused on my young developing bosoms. He’s pretending to be pushing me away but his hands are stuck on me. This makes me uncomfortable. I jump behind.

After all the words and uncouth behavior of Robert’s guards, they finally let me in.

Robert is seated and swinging on his chair caring less that an innocent man is going to be killed. He sips his coffee, puts it down and picks a pen then turns to talk to me.

“Kid, what brings you here?”

“Can you reopen Mr. Ted’s case please?” At this time, there was no reason or point of greetings and salutations.

“Haah!” he gives a mocking laugh.

I already knew what was happening would happen. And I’m sure things are on track.

“Opening the case is not going to change anything. It’ll only delay it.”

I’m beginning to smile inside because that delay is what I want. I need two more weeks to make sure what I’m planning happens.

“He is my brother. Be fair to him, please.”

The Constitution that I now have free access to indicates that Robert listens to kids despite where they are from. I’m young and so that gives me an advantage. And the fact that I am a girl, gives me a two-lead advantage.

I have given myself to be a lawyer. I know very little about law but given that I have read the Constitution, I’m sure I can do this. Every other lawyer who tried to defend my brother ended up dead. They were all killed and so I know I’m in danger. An imminent danger.

I’m looking at the pictures on his office walls and I’m seeing a photo of his son. He’s probably 16 years old. And since schools have closed, I’m sure he’s at home. I’m hoping that he has his father’s DNA of sexual excitement. I don’t like him. I don’t love him but I need him. I’m sure he’s not going to reject me. I’m the black chocolate type. And men are so easy to lure. I need to pretend to love him, that way, he could convince her father based on everything I tell him.

Within the silence, I see Robert walking through his computer. He’s probably sending a message to someone. I’m not sure who but it must be the judge. He’s feeding the judge what to say, how the court should re-decide on my brother’s fate. I don’t care what he says to the judge. He turns to me.

“Listening is scheduled for the day after tomorrow,” he says “Thanks, sir!”

With that, my brother won’t be executed until the court decides. This will take two weeks. I’ve added my brother two more weeks to live. My first step in saving him is successful.

I’m leaving the office straight to look for Robert’s son. It’s time to fall in love.
