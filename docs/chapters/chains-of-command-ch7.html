Chapter Seven

Names Wes

Fiction • 5 min read • Updated Mar 2026

Akinyi and her mother sat on the couch opposite each other. They stared at the TV blankly. None of them is following Jason Statham’s Beekeeper movie. The punches were thrown as Jason wanted to take down the scammers. Nothing mattered at that time but the thoughts of rescuing one of their own. Home felt lonely. It felt like a jungle deserted by all animals.

As the silence consumed them and added depression on top of depression, Akinyi picked up her phone to call her sister Sarah. Her first call went unanswered. She tried again hoping that it’d be answered. Luckily, she answered.

“Hello sis,” she started
“Hello, how are you?” Sarah’s voice echoed from the phone.
“Not fine. Schools have closed, when are you coming home?”
“I’m not coming home this holiday,”
“Why? Something has come up that you need to know and help us gather thoughts,”
“What that?”
“Teds in prison. Waiting for his execution.”
“I’m sorry,” she feigned her empathy.

They talked but the conversation headed nowhere. As Sarah always did to her sister, she gave her history of HPW Walls. That’s the least she wanted to hear from her sister at the moment. She thought that maybe her sister could suggest something straight and helpful.

“Thanks for the history lesson,” Akinyi said as she hung up the phone. Nothing worked with her sister. She was left alone with her mother.

She turned to her mother and remembered the last time she saw her smile. She felt heavy for her.

“I’ll be back, ma’am,” she said as she walked out.

She took the route to Robert’s home. It was 2 pm and she was sure he was still in his office swinging and doing nothing, just waiting for the high taxes to devour and pay his luxuries. The sun scorched her dark skin as sweat rolled down her face burning her delicate eyes. She wiped them occasionally with her white handkerchief. She wanted to look presentable to win.

A truck rustled towards her, throwing clouds of dust all over the place. She ran and hid behind the bush to keep off the dust from her clothes. When it had all settled, she embarked on her journey. She occasionally wiped her face and leaked her lips to keep them pink and moist. She was smart and she needed just a little more composure to make it happen.

Her destination was three kilometers away. She had covered half the distance when a pickup truck offered her a ride. The truck driver was a nice-looking old man. She was sure he had no ill intention. She got in.

“Where to, my daughter?” he asked as he opened the drawer to take out a bottle of sealed water. “Have this,” he added. “Thanks. Just a mile away.” Akinyi replies opening the bottle to take a sip to quench her thirst. “Thanks again Mr.?” “Charles.”
“I’m Jackie.”

In five minutes, the track pulled over and she alighted. “Thanks, Charles.” And the truck drove off.

She knew it would be impossible to get into the compound, let alone the house. She sat under a tree to execute her thoughts and ideas. Firstly, she needed to look loving and admirable. Her voice was sweet so that wasn’t much to be worked on. She wiped her face regularly but gently. Secondly, she needed to act calm and not suspicious. She knew it was only by using a thief that you could catch a thief. She went for Robert’s son.

She composed herself for twenty minutes then walked to the gate. She didn’t press the bell. She looked around and before she could call out, something caught her eye. It was a phone number. She didn’t know whose contact it was. But she was sure it wasn’t Robert’s. On the day she downloaded the constitution, she had also searched for Robert’s possible phone numbers. The one pinned on the gate wasn’t one of them. She decided to dial it in. The voice on the phone sounded like a young man’s voice. Could it be Robert’s son? She thought. But since she was convincing, she went for it. “Hello, this is Jackie,” she started. “I am at the gate and I need a little help. What do you know about these shattered gate lights?” she picked a stone and busted out one of the gate bulbs. “I’ll be out in a second,” the man on the phone assured. When he opened the gate, he looked to see who he had been talking to but couldn’t see anyone. Jackie was watching from a distance to confirm if it was truly him. And yes, it was him. It was the same person she had seen his photo at Robert’s office. He turned to get back in when Akinyi called out. “Hey, it was me that called...uhm I went to excuse myself a little.” “It’s okay. And thanks for letting me know of this. I’ll tell my father to fix it.” “Okay. Wait, can I talk to you for a second please?” “Sure.” “I’m Jackie by the way. And you are?” “I’m John.” “Nice to meet you, John. I was just wondering if you could come closer, please. I can’t go in. Your father will see me on his CCTV,” she said jokingly. “It’s alright,” he said as he moved closer to Jackie. Trepidation gripped Akinyi as she thought of a way to pull it off. She had come a long way to fail, she went for it. “John, you look nice and I’m suggesting that if it’s okay with you, we can go out someday. Have a coffee maybe.” John, who has never been invited to a date gave in, immediately. Akinyi stretched her hand to give him a hug which John embraced with no doubt. ‘Someone has finally liked me,’ I’m sure he said. And it was all going well. Desperate moments call for desperate actions. “Bye. I’ll call you,” said Jackie. “Bye.” John found love. Jackie celebrated as she went back home. She found her mother ready. It was another visitation day and they were to visit one of their own. Nothing could be said at this time. Ted watched his family with fear. He feared he was going to lose them. “Sis, what did you do?” “I had to.” Their mother didn’t know what they were talking about. “What are you talking about?” she interrupted. “Execution has been delayed by two weeks. They gave me a paper which said it.” Mother looked confusedly happy. “What? Who did it” “I did Mum, I pleaded with Robert. He agreed to re-check my brother’s case. And tomorrow we are going to court. I’m representing my brother. I know I can’t win but I just need to buy time.” They talked their normal family talk. The end of the visitation bell went off and it was time to go. Jackie hugged her brother and they left. Tomorrow was going to be a big and tiresome day.
