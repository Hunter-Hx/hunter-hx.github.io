Chapter Four

Names Wes

Fiction • 5 min read • Updated Mar 2026

When the pastor at HPW Walls finished his work of the day- preaching for the convicts, Ted remained behind to ask for forgiveness. Like everyone would do, he was cleaning and mopping his path with his tears before meeting his creator. He knelt at the altar with chains all over him. At HPW Walls, convicts are chained every time as they claim that some might escape from the church corridor which is impossible since watch towers are active at all times. And the fences sound an alarm if anyone, other than the guards, gets close by 2 feet. It’s nearly impossible to break out.

Silence filled the room. It was only Ted, Sam, the pews, and the angels they believed were there. It was so quiet that you could hear someone breathe. The silence reminded Ted of something; he had seven more days to live. Seven days to leave this world forever. Seven days to be forever quiet. He knew the chair was set. Power wasn’t going to be a problem. He therefore needed to clean up his slate. He wanted it to be clear. He didn’t want any blemish. But what broke him the most, was that he was confessing to something he hadn’t done. He was asking for forgiveness for something someone he didn’t know did.

Dan watched him with disgust in his eyes. He hated Ted. Their conversations weren’t always on track. “You killed a man, you killed a man,” he started, “why are you asking for forgiveness, huh? Let’s go!” “Just a minute, badge,” Ted requested.
“One minute.”

But one minute was not enough for him to ask for everything he wanted. He had failed his mother and he knew the society was now on her. He had always been her protector. They enjoyed her downfall. One thing, however, rose in his mind, he knew all this would have been prevented had he had the courage to tell his mother how his sister used to quench her sexual lusts. Nothing worked at the moment. A 23-year-old was going to die.

He mumbled his prayer. “Father Lord, forgive me for I have become unworthy of being on this earth.” And for the first time in eight years, Ted prayed.

It was time to go. Time to go back into the dark cage.
* * * * * * *

When Ted’s sister, Akinyi, closed for her school holidays, she knew a lot of good things were waiting for her at home. Her brother had promised her lots of gifts and outings, to eat foods of her choice, and visit places of her choosing. She loved Ted.

So, when she got home, the first question after she hugged her mother was where his brother was. “Where’s my brother? I missed him.” Mother knew she had a lot to explain. Before she could get the response, she so highly needed, she spotted sadness register on her mother’s face. “Your brother won’t be joining us,” she responded. “Why? Is he sick?”
“No. He’s in prison. HPW Walls.”

The name of the prison frightened her. She had heard some stories of that prison. Her school’s deskmate had told her the story of her father being sentenced to that same prison and never coming back home. He was murdered and his corpse was thrown down into the river, he was never recovered. Their history teacher had also given them a little history of the prison a day before school closed. Everything she had heard about the prison frightened her and hearing that his brother was at HPW Walls, that very prison made tears form in her eyes, flowing down her chubby cheeks. “Is he working in the prison?”
“No. He’s been sentenced.”
“What? Ted? My brother?” she asked confusedly.
“I’m sorry, dear,” her mother tried to comfort her, even though she was almost bursting into tears.

“Ted?” she called faintly.
“Mr. Robert imprisoned him,” her mother added
“No way. Why would he? No! No! I want to see my brother.” “You can’t see him today. Visitation is tomorrow. I’ll take you.” “Oh, brother.”
What Akinyi hoped would be fun turned out to be one of the worst nightmares she’s ever had. Her only brother is in prison. The words had sounded more of a lie than they were. But no, Ted was in prison, waiting his last days. Akinyi didn’t sleep all night. The thought of her brother in cuffs made her heart bleed. She felt for him. The next day was visitation day. Ted’s name was called signaling that he had a visitor. Sam, walked to his hole to fish him out. He opened the door and walked him out. He’d been in the dark for 24 hours. It felt great to see the sun, again. Sam added one more handcuff to confirm that he couldn’t escape whatsoever. “You got a visitor,” he said and walked him to where his family was waiting for him. Akinyi watched his brother through the glasses as he was being escorted towards them. Once a free man, now in chains with guards following him. She hated what reality was doing to her brother. The door opened and her tears poured out endlessly. She rushed to hug her brother. She hated what they had done to her brother. She hated the chains. She then hated the guards. She hated HPW Walls. She hated Robert’s state. But she knew the hugs and the cries weren’t enough. She wanted her brother out of those walls. She wanted their family to disappear from Robert’s state; a place they loathed with their whole lives. Their mother couldn’t say a word. Akinyi and his brother walked to a bench at the far corner to talk about what happened. She saw the guard following them and she got annoyed. “Won’t you leave my brother for just five minutes? Urgh!” she expressed her frustration with the boring pursuit and unlawful behavior. The guard stepped aside, five meters for five minutes. His eyes glued on Ted. He always thought he could escape. A family of three, seated on prison benches. They should have been four but one had become a stranger. “It’s good to see you,” Ted clouded his fear with a smile. “Ted, there's nothing good here,” “But there’s nothing I can do,” “No, Mum and I will bail you out,” loving Akinyi assured his brother. Ted turned to his mother, “So you haven’t told her? “Tell me what?” Akinyi asked eyes out. “They are executing me in six days, dear,” “What?” “Yeah.” “That’s not possible. What did you do to deserve this?” “I don’t want you to hate me.” “No! Tell me.” Ted explained to her sister what had happened. How he got there and what has been going on ever since he got inside those walls. He, however, didn’t tell her who framed him. That could break the little girl to death. He cared. When she heard Ted say that the deputy governor was dead, she knew that wasn’t true. His brother wasn’t a murderer. “Deputy Governor, Mr. John? He’s alive.” “How do you know?” “He was with our principal two days ago. I was in the office when he showed up. When the principal saw me, he chased me out which signaled that something was wrong. He’s never chased me out of his office before. Never.” Ted assumed that maybe her sister saw someone that looked like him. “I swear. It was him.” As they were talking about the grief that had fallen them, their mother had been quiet all along. She was consumed with fear of losing her son. She watched her children consumed with despair. She called one guard. “Can I talk to you deputy, please?” “She’s not around,” the guard responded humbly. “When she comes, can you talk to her for me? Tell her it's Ted’s mother.” “Okay, ma’am.” Michael, the most respectful guard responded. It got to a level where a mother was begging to talk to her daughter who had turned her back away from everyone in the family. This felt heavy for the poor mother. Her tears showed as she wiped them away with the white handkerchief handed to her by her daughter. Akinyi was hopeful and smart. She thought of calling her elder sister, Sarah, to tell her that their brother was in prison. She thought she might help since Sarah was a teacher and schools were closed. “Sarah can help,” she said. Ted and his mother did not respond. They cared. The end of the visitation bell rang and it was time to go. Akinyi hugged her brother and watched him being escorted back to his hole. She felt lonely even though she was with her mother. Her brother was not one of them. As they walked out, Akinyi walked to one guard to talk to him. “Do you guys have a stand-by generator?” she asked uninterestedly “No. HPW Walls electricity is powered from the sources. There has never been a blackout,” the guard responded. “Okay, thanks.” Her mother who was already out waited for her. “What were you asking the guard?” “Nothing. Just if they have a standby generator and they don’t. Mom, they aren’t going to execute my brother. No.” “My child, they are bigger than anyone. Nothing is going to hold them back.” “I will.” “How?” “I don’t know how but I will,” she concluded.
