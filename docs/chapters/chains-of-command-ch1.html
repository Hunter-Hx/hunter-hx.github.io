Chapter One

Names Wes

Fiction • 5 min read • Updated Apr 2026

When Ted woke up on his seventh day after conviction- his weary figure behind the walls of a state penitentiary- he knew his death was only 13 days away before 120 volts of electricity passed inside his veins with his face covered with black silken clothing, water soaked on a sponge and fixed on his head, roll one as he dries like corn. There was no way back for what had come his way. He was just a poor man from the streets, he wasn’t going to win and even if he was to win, they wouldn’t allow it. And with ‘they’, I mean the invisible government’s hand. As it always was in Robert’s state; I do things my way, if you are happy, build your state, if you are not, go to hell. The notice that he had pinned on his office door. 13 days to live. 13 days to be killed for something I didn’t do, he said to himself.

Robert’s state was a mixed-up world. It was where a rapist seated in a court chair holding a gavel would sentence another rapist for not using a condom; where a briber would sentence another briber for taking too much. In short, it’s where a white man’s dog bites a black man’s dog for not acting decent, despite them all being dogs.

For the seven days, Ted had been at HPW Walls, convicted killers on the execution wait list reduced rapidly. Execution was done daily! Ted and his fellow convicts knew what was in store for them- death and there was no way they were going to escape. How daring could one be to try and escape a state penitentiary under 24-hour surveillance? They knew death awaited them but that did not guarantee them to risk being gunned down. Despite all, Ted had a little faith. Even though the reality was crushing his faith. Just have a little faith, words of his mother echoed from within him. Seated on the chapel pews, holding a bible and a prayer magazine, Ted reflected on how his past days had been. How the judge, his neighbor, had sentenced him to death. “It’s a major crime, Mr. Ted. You’ve been found guilty and I can’t do anything other than to sentence you to death by electrocution. You’ll be taken to a state penitentiary as you await your execution, I’m sorry,” Mr. Brown had said. The words I’m sorry were unnecessary, I bet he said it to show a little sympathy. Something that was never within him. The judge had been a saboteur; destroying his neighbor, deliberately. Dang! Dang! The sound of the judge’s gavel was final. Level one penitentiary isn’t a joke. It’s where rapists and ministers dine together. As Gicheru said, it’s a house for all! Ted couldn’t have done anything. The government was final. They are always final. As the pastor read them a verse, for everything there’s a reason, and a time for every matter under heaven; a time to be born and a time to die… Ted knew his case wasn’t considered. He wasn’t supposed to be where they’d taken him. He…

Escorted from the courthouse to prison; three armed men on both sides, two behind and one in front, his legs chained to the cuffs as the jingling of the chains swayed with him. A convicted murderer matched to the waiting convoy to land unknown; to be executed. His mother watched him as tears dropped from her eyes. She felt the pain of being a mother. “Please, I beg you to be lenient to my son,” she had pleaded with the judge. Mr. Brown had folded his nose to show how disgusting her words had been.

“Leniency is death,” he had said.

She knew she was never going to see her son again. Or maybe so, she thought.

When they reached the prison, Ted was isolated on his own and thrown into a hole where only a little light crept in. Before they kill you, they torture your mind. He was considered the most dangerous. He had killed a state’s deputy governor.

“You know what buddy, you went too far,” Sam, his guard started, “You should have killed these rapists that devour our dignity. But a deputy governor? I’d be happy to roll one,” he said as he demonstrated how Ted was going to die on the chair. A helpless man amidst beasts. “Welcome to HPW Walls.”
Ted watched him throw every dirty word at him. The world had isolated itself from him.
“So, I deserve to die?”
“Yeh buddy! We should skip some people to get you on the chair very fast.”
“Is that what men do to other men in your world, man killing a man to impress the state?”
“You know you are the most dangerous here. We’ve never had a deputy governor’s killer. Pride killer!” He said as he closed the door, letting darkness again into the room. “When I get out of here, you’ll be my first victim.”
“You’re never getting out buddy. Maybe in a casket.”
One thing about the guards at the HPW Walls is that they were so consumed by the government that they never had morals anymore. They were working to impress, not to enforce the law. They were much more into killing than rehabilitation. Meanwhile, as Ted was facing the harsh treatment at the prison, his mother back at home felt unwanted. She desperately wanted to rescue her son. Her appeals and requests had fallen on deaf ears. Appeal papers and shreds of evidence had been destroyed. The lawyers were been hunted down one by one and murdered. Every witness in Ted’s case was in danger. The government wanted a perfect and smooth operation with no setbacks. As the saying goes…. Ted’s mother knew she was in danger, too, but that didn’t stop her from trying for her son. She visited the governor’s office to seek clemency, and she was lucky enough to be let into Robert’s office. “What brings a stinking woman into my office this morning?” “My son, Ted” she responded trying to hide the tears that were already showing. “What’s with him? Isn’t he supposed to be executed?” “I’ve come to ask a pardon on his behalf, please,” she pleaded “That’s something I can’t do to a convicted killer. Get out of my office!” He thundered “Please,” she cried “Guards! Come pick this unworthy creature out of my office. She’s a disgrace.” Two guards hurriedly opened the door and held the poor woman by her sleeves. They didn’t feel anything for her. Why would they? She was just an unworthy creature, mother of a killer. They threw her outside and left her to stagger from the excessive force. “That was quite nice,” one guard said. “Yeah! It’s good to be on the dominant side,” his stupid colleague responded. She walked out down to the gate in shame. She regretted being a mother, but at most, she regretted being a member of that state, of Robert’s state. I have failed him, she cried. Even though Robert knew the woman was right, he wasn’t going to lend the clemency. He sat in his office filled with pride in being a leader. A leader. “Don’t let any other fool inside my office again,” he warned the guards. “Okay, sir!” He grabbed his phone, to talk to his wife. “Could you come to my office for a minute, darling?” Mr. Robert, fool of the state. He’d fuck your woman and if you complain, you’d eat beans for the rest of your life. Men feared a man. When his wife got to where she hated the most, Robert’s office, she knew something was up. “You never let me in, what’s it this time?” his wife asked boldly. “I need to tell you something,” Caroline held her head with her hands, she knew he either wanted her to help him with her wits or just a favor. Caroline was always human and his husband was just a human being. Robert narrated the boring novel of Ted’s mother. “Okay.” She said as she left the office. Ted’s mother, slumped on a couch at her home, didn’t know what to do next. She had tried the only possible way. “There’s no way out of this,” she whispered. But she wasn’t going to let her son die. She was going to try all possible ways, even if it meant taking her own life. Her thoughts consumed her letting her into the slumberland, a peaceful world for a short while. At HPW Walls, all convicted killers were gathered together, chained from legs to waists to wrists. They were five, five dangerous men. They were chatting about the whys and how they got into HPW Walls. One thin guy, they called him Wato started, “I’m here for armed robbery that led to the death of six people. I also killed my neighbor. But all those things just happened, I didn’t intend them to.” “You’re saying it’s black magic?” Dave, the youngest asked funnily. “Yeah.” “I’m not supposed to be here, but you know, Mr. Robert, he slept with my wife and... I don’t have to tell you what you already know. He would have sentenced me to life but I once killed his dog. This landed me to execution.” As everyone was telling their stories, Ted was consumed with the thought of whether his mother was safe. His mind wasn’t in the talk when Wato tapped him, “Why are you here?” “I killed a man. Not just a man but a deputy governor.” He knew it wasn’t going to do him any good to start narrating the planted story of the murder. After all, they couldn’t help him. They were all convicts. Blind men don’t lend each other their walking rods. “Everyone back to their hole,” Sam yelled. As Ted was walking back to his hole, he spotted someone that looked like his elder sister. “Excuse me, badge, who’s that?” He asked as he pointed at his sister. “That’s the deputy of this penitentiary” “What’s her name?”
“Why do you want to know?”
“Just tell me her name, I’m curious.”
“She’s Sarah Aila.”
“Aila?” He asked eyes popped.
“You look surprised.”
“It’s nothing,” Ted played calmly.

The name was her sister’s and his eyes had not deceived him.

When he sat in his hole, he remembered what her sister told them she was doing. Where she was working.

“I’m a teacher,” she had said. And since then, everyone in the family thought she was a teacher. Even her mother thought she was a teacher. She’s a teacher, she said with a smile. With sadness on his face, Ted tried to tie up some strings, to join some dots. If my sister is the deputy here, that means she knows Robert or maybe works for him. Robert the guy that made me land here. My sister could be behind all this. As he thought, his mind ran into the day before he was captured. His sister had called him to help with some jobs. That could have raised something up but since Ted needed a job and it came from his sister, he knew it was legit even though his sister hated him. She had called. “Hey Chicko, I need you to do something for me.” He didn’t want to think of anything beyond that. They have had a rough past. Days were moving quickly and his execution day was closer. On the past four days he had been there, his sister didn’t want to come any closer to him. She was always trying by all means not to meet him. Whenever Ted sent a guard requesting to talk to the deputy, she’d turn it down.

Two siblings, one a deputy and the other her prisoner. When Ted’s mother came on the visitation day, she looked at his son. She saw his innocence. An innocent man was cuffed while talking to his mother. “It’ll pass mum, it always does.”
“No! I must do something. I went to the governor’s office and he chased me out like a dog.”
“Have you talked to your daughter?”
“My daughter?”
“Yeah, Sarah.”
“Yes! She told me how she’s swamped with classes. Exams are approaching and she’s very busy.”
“No mum, she’s here.”
“What?”
“Your daughter, she’s the deputy here. She’s working for Robert.”
“How do you know?”
“I saw her, mum. With my eyes.”
“That can’t be true. She’s a teacher.”
“She lied mum, she lied.”
